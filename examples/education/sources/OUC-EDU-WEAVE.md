W—Wonder: Why we design this activity？
Starting from our project and feedback from previous activities, we consider who
we aim to reach, why we choose this audience, and why we focus on particular
educational topics and directions. By identifying their educational needs, questions,
and knowledge gaps, we establish the purpose and direction of each activity.
E—Explore: How can we design for meaningful learning?
We explore objective-driven design to define clear learning goals and navigate how
scientific content, communication methods, and interactive formats can be
adapted to different age groups.
A—Action: How do we bring the design to life?
We translate our objectives into practical actions online and offline.
V—Verify: What did participants gain?
Through participant feedback and other forms of evaluation, we examine whether the activity
achieved its intended objectives and what participants gained from it.
E — Echo: What can we improve next?
We follow a Feedback–Reflection–Evolution cycle: collecting participant feedback, reflecting
on the effectiveness and limitations of both educational content and activity design, and
translating these insights into improvements. These improvements inform subsequent iterations,
where they can be implemented, evaluated, and further refined.
